Preparation steps

    What does the purity (% w/w) of a reagent mean?

    The purity, usually printed on the label as the assay, is the mass fraction of the bottle contents that is the compound of interest. Hydrochloric acid at 37 % w/w means that every 100 g of the concentrated acid contains 37 g of HCl and 63 g of water. It is a percentage by mass, not a molarity: to dilute it you first need the density to convert it to mol/L.

    Formula

    First, calculate the concentration of the reagent as it comes in the bottle:

    Stock concentration

    Cstock (mol/L) = (ρ × 1000 × P / 100) / M

    Where ρ = density (g/mL), P = purity (% w/w) and M = molar mass (g/mol).

    Then apply C₁V₁ = C₂V₂:

    Volume to measure

    Vmeasure (mL) = (Cfinal × Vfinal) / Cstock × 1000

    With Cfinal in mol/L and Vfinal in liters.

    Worked example: 500 mL of 1 M HCl from 37 % HCl

    HCl 37 % w/w, density 1.19 g/mL, M = 36.46 g/mol

    1. Molarity of the concentrated acid: Cstock = (1.19 × 1000 × 37 / 100) / 36.46 = 12.08 mol/L

    2. Volume to measure: V = (1.00 mol/L × 500 mL) / 12.08 mol/L = 41.4 mL

    3. In a fume hood, measure the acid with a graduated cylinder, add it slowly to about 250 mL of distilled water in a 500 mL volumetric flask, let it cool and make up to the mark.

    Quick reference for 37 % HCl (density 1.19 g/mL):

    Frequently asked questions

    How much 37 % HCl do I need to make 1 M HCl?

    With typical concentrated HCl (37 % w/w, density 1.19 g/mL), the stock is about 12.08 mol/L. You need about 8.3 mL for 100 mL of 1 M HCl, 41.4 mL for 500 mL and 82.8 mL for 1 L. For 1 L of 0.1 M HCl, about 8.3 mL is enough. Use the assay and density on your own bottle for the exact volume.

    Why do you always add acid to water and not the other way around?

    Concentrated acids and bases release a lot of heat when they mix with water. If you pour water onto the acid, the heat can boil the small amount of water violently and spatter acid. When you add the acid to the water, the large mass of water absorbs the heat safely.

    Where do I find the density and the purity?

    On the bottle label, on the supplier's certificate of analysis (CoA), or in the safety data sheet (SDS) available on the manufacturer's website (Sigma-Aldrich, Merck, etc.). The purity is usually listed as the assay, in % w/w.
